About this redesign

This page explains why each part of the new homepage exists, why it’s placed where it is, and what was taken out. The redesign is an independent, unofficial proposal by Parphool Kumar Yadav.

Principles

Phone first
Most people in Nepal browse on phones and mobile data. Every page is designed for a 375 px screen first.
Light pages
The homepage is about 250 KB, down from 6.2 MB, and loads no JavaScript. It opens quickly even on a weak 3G signal.
Text, not images of text
Notices can be read, searched, translated and read aloud by screen readers.
Nepali and English
Every page exists in both languages. The button in the header switches to the same page in the other language.
Accessible
There’s one main heading per page, keyboard navigation with visible focus, good colour contrast, and a “skip to content” link.

The homepage, section by section

  1. 1

    Unofficial-concept banner

    Very top of every page

    Why it’s there: This is a proposal, not the council’s site. Saying so on every page, with a link to the official site, means nobody mistakes it for the real thing or submits anything here.

    Why it’s placed here: It sits above everything so it’s seen before anything else. It’s one thin line, so it doesn’t push the content down.

  2. 2

    Header: name, six links and language button

    Top, sticky while scrolling

    Why it’s there: The council’s name appears in the page’s language, with the other language underneath. The menu has six plain labels instead of the current nine menus with sub-menus. The नेपाली / English button switches the whole page to the other language.

    Why it’s placed here: The header stays visible while scrolling, so people can always get back to services or notices, or switch language, on a long page. On phones the menu collapses into one “Menu” button that works even without JavaScript.

  3. 3

    Hero: one sentence on what the council does

    First screen

    Why it’s there: Students, parents and members of the public who land here from Google need to know in one line what the Nepal Pharmacy Council is. Two buttons send them to the two things most people come for: online services and notices.

    Why it’s placed here: It’s the first thing seen, and it replaces the current photo slider. Most visitors never look past the first slide of a slider, and it was downloading five large photos (about 820 KB) before anything else.

  4. 4

    Urgent-notice banner

    Directly under the hero

    Why it’s there: Urgent notices, such as the warning about fake exam-preparation classes or certificate collection dates, must be seen. This banner replaces the current pop-up, which covered the page with an image of a scanned letter that can’t be read on a phone.

    Why it’s placed here: It overlaps the bottom of the hero so it’s visible without scrolling on most phones. It picks the latest notice marked “Urgent” automatically, so staff don’t have to update the homepage by hand. Unlike a pop-up, it never blocks the page.

  5. 5

    Online services

    First main section

    Why it’s there: Most visits are to do one job: apply for the licensing exam, renew registration, check a result, verify a pharmacist, or get a Good Standing letter or NOC. Each card gives the name in both languages and one line on what it does, with an icon showing it opens the official portal.

    Why it’s placed here: This is the most-used part of the site, so it comes first. The cards are ordered by how often people need them: exam, renewal and results come first. On phones the descriptions are hidden so all eight services fit in about two screens.

  6. 6

    Latest notices

    Second section, left column

    Why it’s there: Notices are the second most common reason to visit. Each item shows the type (Urgent, Result, Notice, Event), the date in both AD and BS, and the title. Every notice opens as readable text, not a scanned image.

    Why it’s placed here: It comes right after services because people check it often, especially around exams. Only the latest four are shown, with a link to the full list, so the homepage stays short.

  7. 7

    Registration numbers and “Is your pharmacist registered?”

    Second section, right column (below notices on phones)

    Why it’s there: The numbers (8,563 pharmacists and 17,692 pharmacist assistants) show the scale of the register at a glance. The Know Your Pharmacist box speaks to the public, not professionals: it tells people how to check that whoever gives them medicines is qualified, which is a key part of the council’s role in public safety.

    Why it’s placed here: It sits beside the notices as a sidebar, so it’s visible but never competes with the main tasks. On phones it moves below the notices.

  8. 8

    About the council and contact card

    Last section before the footer

    Why it’s there: A short summary of the council’s role, with a link to the full About page, and the address, phone numbers and email, which people can tap to call or write.

    Why it’s placed here: Fewer visitors need this, so it’s lower on the page. The contact details are also repeated in the footer on every page.

  9. 9

    Footer

    Bottom of every page

    Why it’s there: Contact details, the five most-used services, and site links, so every page has a way forward.

    Why it’s placed here: People who scroll to the end of any page are usually looking for contact details or a way out, and the footer gives them both.

What was removed, and why

RemovedReason
Photo slider (5 photos, ~820 KB)Heavy, rarely looked at past the first slide, and it pushed the services down.
Board photos (2 MB each)Replaced with names and initials on the About page. If photos come back, they should be under 30 KB each.
Pop-up with a scanned letterBlocked the page and couldn’t be read on phones. Replaced by the urgent-notice banner.
Scrolling news tickerMoving text is hard to read, it couldn’t be paused, and it fails accessibility rules (WCAG 2.2.2).
Animated flag GIF (250 KB)Decoration only.
Facebook widgetLoads third-party trackers and a lot of script. It’s replaced by a simple link to the Facebook page.
Expired exam-form box and payment QR codeThe notice had closed, and the QR code image was 566 KB. Payment details now belong in the relevant notice.
